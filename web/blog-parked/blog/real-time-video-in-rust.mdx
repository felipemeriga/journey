At 60 frames per second you get **16.7 milliseconds** per frame. Everything —
decode, process, encode, deliver — has to fit inside that window, every frame,
or the viewer sees it. This is the constraint that shapes every decision in a
real-time video pipeline.

<YouTube id="aqz-KE-bpKQ" title="Demo: the frame-budget readout in action" />

## Where the time goes

On the interactive player at Cosm, a single frame runs through roughly:

- **Decode** the 8K fisheye source (hardware NVDEC)
- **Process** — crop the virtual camera, dewarp the lens distortion (CUDA)
- **Encode** the output viewport (NVENC)
- **Deliver** over WebRTC

Keep the frame on the GPU the whole way through and you avoid the single most
expensive thing you can do in this loop: copying frames back and forth between
CPU and GPU memory.

## Why Rust

When the budget is this tight, you can't afford a garbage-collection pause or a
surprise allocation in the hot path. Rust gives you predictable, no-GC control
over memory and strong guarantees around concurrency — exactly what you want when
the deadline is 16.7 ms and it never moves.

> The whole discipline of real-time media is: know your budget, measure against it
> every frame, and move expensive work off the critical path.
